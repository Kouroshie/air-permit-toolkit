A five-minute tour

Five stops that show what the toolkit does. Each link opens a calculator with the inputs already filled in, so you can read the result and then change any number.

Tool: kouroshie.github.io/air-permit-toolkitRuns in: any browser, nothing uploaded
Stop 1Boiler

The maximum day, not the annual average

Open the boiler example: 5 MMBtu/hr, NOx 0.10 lb/MMBtu, 24 hr/day, 2,000 hr/yr.

NOx is 0.5 lb/hr and 0.5 ton/yr, but the boiler can run all day, so the highest day is 12 lb. That is above the 10 lb/day threshold in Regulation 2-2-301, so the page flags BACT for NOx. An annual hours limit lowers the annual total, not the daily one. Scroll down to the Calculation trail to see every step with its units.

Stop 2Generator

Units, and two flags at once

Open the generator example: 400 bhp, NOx 1.5 g/bhp-hr, 50 hr/yr.

NOx is 1.323 lb/hr, 31.75 lb on a 24-hour day and 66.1 lb/yr. Two flags appear: BACT for NOx and CO, and diesel particulate at 0.97 lb/yr, above the 0.26 lb/yr chronic trigger in Table 2-5-1, so a Rule 2-5 health risk assessment is needed.

Now change Rating unit to Electrical kW (kWe). The page converts through generator efficiency (400 ÷ 0.95 ÷ 0.7457 = 564.6 bhp) and every value rises by about 41%. Entering kWe as horsepower is a common error.

Stop 3Toxics

From pounds per year to cancer risk

Open the toxics example: the generator's diesel PM, 0.97 lb/yr.

With the Air District CEQA screening factor (360 µg/m³ per g/s) and the OEHHA 2015 exposure equations, the residential cancer risk is 3.74 in a million: above the TBACT trigger of 1.0 in Rule 2-5-301 and within the project limit of 10 in Rule 2-5-302. Tick Project is in an Overburdened Community and the limit becomes 6. This is a screening estimate; a refined assessment uses AERMOD and HARP 2.

Stop 4Fees

Which minimum applies

Open the fee example: the generator's fee basis of 2.8 MMBtu/hr, first toxic source, toxic surcharge.

Filing $684, initial $573, risk assessment $1,379, permit to operate $407 and toxic surcharge $40.70, for $3,083.70. For a source this small, each Schedule B fee is set by its minimum, and the trail shows the comparison.

Stop 5Control

Capture limits control

Open the control example: 90% capture, 95% control.

Overall control is 0.90 × 0.95 = 85.5%, so 14.5% remains, not 5%. Type 90 in Second device in series: overall control becomes 89.55%, because the second device only treats what the hood captures. Overall control can never exceed capture efficiency.

Limits. A screening and teaching tool, not a permitting tool; its results are not permit decisions. No building downwash, terrain or real weather data, so it cannot replace AERMOD and HARP 2. It does not check prohibitory limits in Regulations 8 and 9, and fees change every July. Independent open-source project, not affiliated with or endorsed by the Bay Area Air District.
