Common pitfalls, and how the tool handles them

PitfallTool
"An annual-hours limit does not automatically reduce the maximum daily rate."Daily values use hours per day; annual hours only change lb/yr. The boiler shows 12 lb/day at 2,000 hr/yr.
"Evaluate BACT applicability; the arithmetic alone does not establish the full regulatory result."Flags BACT for boiler NOx (12 lb/day) and generator NOx and CO, citing 2-2-301.
"Do not substitute electrical kW for mechanical hp without the appropriate conversion."A rating unit menu converts kWe through generator efficiency: 400 kWe ÷ 0.95 ÷ 0.7457 = 564.6 bhp.
"Residual emissions are 14.5%, not 5%. Never apply an efficiency twice."Overall control is capture × device efficiency; a second device acts only on the captured stream, so control never exceeds capture.
"Match gas reference conditions."The flare uses 379.5 scf/lb-mol at 60 °F; Method 19 uses 385.3 at 68 °F. Both are stated in the trail.
HRA chain: emissions → dispersion → concentration → exposure and toxicity → risk.The toxics page follows that chain step by step (see example 6).

Example 1: boiler

Inputs: 5 MMBtu/hr, NOx 0.10 lb/MMBtu, 24 hr/day, 2,000 hr/yr, natural gas.

Worked answer: 0.50 lb/hr; 12 lb/day; 1,000 lb/yr = 0.50 tons/yr.

Tool: 0.5 lb/hr; 12 lb/day (orange, at or above the trigger); 1,000 lb/yr; 0.5 ton/yr. Flag: BACT is triggered for NOx under 2-2-301.

The other pollutants use the AP-42 Section 1.4 defaults, which the example does not set. Open this example: combustion link.

Example 2: diesel generator

Inputs: 400 bhp, NOx 1.5 g/bhp-hr, 24 hr/day worst case, 50 hr/yr.

Worked answer: 1.323 lb/hr; 66.1 lb/yr; 0.0331 short tons; 31.75 lb/day if 24-hour operation is possible.

Tool: 1.323 lb/hr; 66.14 lb/yr; 0.03307 ton/yr; 31.75 lb/day. Flags: BACT for NOx and CO; diesel PM 0.97 lb/yr exceeds the Table 2-5-1 chronic trigger of 0.26 lb/yr, so a Rule 2-5 health risk assessment is required.

CO, POC, PM and SO2 use the tool's Tier 4 Final defaults, which the example does not set. engine link.

Example 3: flare

Inputs: 100 Mscf, 1,000 Btu/scf, NOx factor 0.05 lb/MMBtu.

Worked answer: 100 MMBtu; 5 lb.

Tool: heat released 100 MMBtu; NOx 5 lb (1.25 lb/hr over the default 4-hour event). The trail also gives CO, SO2 from H2S and VOC after 98% destruction, from the default composition.

flare link.

Example 4: control efficiency

Inputs: 100 lb/day uncontrolled, 90% capture, 95% control, no second device.

Worked answer: 85.5% overall; 14.5% remains.

Tool: 85.5%; 14.5 lb/day remain.

One step further: a 90% second device in series gives 0.90 × (1 − 0.05 × 0.10) = 89.55%. Before the independent review, the tool returned 98.55%, more than the 90% capture. That was the one real error found, and it is now fixed and tested.

control link.

Example 5: kilowatts versus horsepower

A common error is to swap electrical kW for mechanical hp. Entering the same 400 as kWe:

Hand check: 400 ÷ 0.95 ÷ 0.7457 = 564.6 bhp.

Tool: "Engine rating used: 564.6 bhp"; NOx 1.867 lb/hr and 44.81 lb/day, about 41% more than the bhp case.

kWe link.

Example 6: carrying the generator into toxics

The health risk chain, using the generator's diesel PM (0.97 lb/yr, 0.0194 lb/hr).

Hand check: 0.97 lb/yr × 1.43833×10⁻⁵ = 1.3952×10⁻⁵ g/s; × 360 = 0.005023 µg/m³; × CPF 1.1 × 676.63×10⁻⁶ residential factor = 3.738 in a million. Chronic HI = 0.005023 / 5 = 0.001005.

Tool: 3.738 in a million; chronic HI 0.001005. TBACT required (above 1.0); within the project limit of 10, and of 6 in an Overburdened Community.

The residential factor equals the District CEQA Health Risk Calculator 5.0 factor (47,364 / 70 × 10⁻⁶). toxics link.
